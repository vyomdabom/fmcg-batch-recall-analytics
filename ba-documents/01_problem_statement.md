# Problem Statement

> Template — write this yourself after discovery. A strong problem statement is 3–5 sentences: situation, problem, impact, and what "solved" looks like. Avoid describing the solution here.

## Draft structure

**Situation:** <!-- Who is FreshRoute Foods, what has changed (growth), what systems are used today -->

**Problem:** <!-- The core problem in one sentence. Anchor: no centralised, reliable link between batches, expiry, supplier documents, orders, complaints, and recall exposure -->

**Impact:** <!-- Quantify where possible from your dataset: hours to trace a batch, % orders missing batch IDs, stock value at risk, expired certificates found. Cover operational, compliance, financial, reputational risk -->

**Desired outcome:** <!-- What the business can do when this is solved, stated as capability not technology (e.g. "trace any batch to customer level within 15 minutes") -->

## Checklist

- [ ] Free of solution language ("needs a database" is a solution, not a problem)
- [ ] At least two impacts quantified from your data
- [ ] Reviewed against the project brief and interview findings
